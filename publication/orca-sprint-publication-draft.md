# ORCA Sprint

An ORCA Sprint is a focused, collaborative workshop format that applies OOUX's Objects, Relationships, Calls-to-Action, and Attributes to a defined design problem. OOUX describes a one-week format involving designers, developers, domain experts, business stakeholders, and content practitioners.

Its purpose is to build shared understanding and useful design artifacts. A sprint can expose questions that require further research or decisions after the workshop.

## What should you prepare?

Choose a bounded problem, such as helping travel agents manage bookings within a trip. Identify the users and outcomes in scope, collect relevant research and existing records, and invite people who can explain the domain and implementation constraints.

Prepare a shared workspace, agree on terminology, and identify who can resolve disputed business rules. Keep assumptions visibly separate from evidence.

## An illustrative five-day agenda

The agenda below is an example adaptation, not a reproduction of an official ORCA Sprint schedule.

| Day | Focus | Useful output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Review the problem and identify candidate objects | Shortlist with definitions, evidence, and open questions. |
| 2 | Explore relationships and user entry points | Nested Object Matrix and initial navigation ideas. |
| 3 | Map actions by role and inspect attributes | CTA Matrix, object map, and unresolved rule questions. |
| 4 | Clarify important requirements and prioritize scope | Agreed decisions, deferred work, and reasons. |
| 5 | Sketch and review a task through the model | Prototype or connected sketches with a validation plan. |

Adjust the sequence when earlier work already answers a question. If evidence is missing, use the workshop to identify how to obtain it.

## Example: resolving a booking ambiguity

Sales and ticketing use “confirmed” differently. During the sprint, the team separates an accepted Offer from a supplier-confirmed Booking.

It records who owns each status, which events change it, and what an agent needs to see at the handoff. The resulting definitions guide the prototype and become questions to validate with users.

## How do you know the sprint helped?

Check whether the team can explain the objects, trace a representative task, and identify the decisions still outstanding. Assign owners and next steps to unresolved questions.

A completed object map records the workshop's model. Validate it through realistic records and user tasks before treating it as a finished solution. Keep the artifacts current as the product changes.
