# Product Strategy Canvas

A product strategy canvas is a one-page tool for making a product’s strategic choices explicit. It helps a team connect the people it serves, their needs, the product’s approach, and the business outcomes it aims to achieve.

## Which questions should a strategy canvas answer?

Templates use different fields. One documented approach is Roman Pichler’s Product Vision Board, which captures a vision, target group, needs, product features, and business goals. Choose a named template or state which questions your team’s version covers so collaborators understand the choices it records.

## Example: A hotel operations product

The following example uses the Product Vision Board’s headings.

| Field | Illustrative choice |
|---|---|
| Vision | Make room-turnover handoffs clear for small hotel teams. |
| Target group | Owners, front-desk staff, and housekeeping teams in small hotels. |
| Needs | Know which rooms need attention and which are ready, with clear responsibility for outstanding work. |
| Product | A shared mobile room-status view, task assignments, and a way to hand over unresolved issues. |
| Business goals | Establish repeat use across shifts and paid renewal after a pilot. |

The team still needs evidence that these needs matter and that the proposed product helps. For example, observe a shift change and test whether staff can identify the next room to prepare and who owns an unresolved issue. Review repeat use and the decision to renew alongside those task outcomes.

## How do you keep the canvas useful?

Mark assumptions, identify the most consequential uncertainty, and choose a discovery activity that can resolve it. Update the canvas when evidence changes a strategic choice. Then use those choices to guide roadmap priorities.

Name related tools precisely. Pichler’s Product Vision Board captures vision and strategy. His separate Product Canvas combines user stories with personas, scenarios, and other UX material for more detailed product work.
