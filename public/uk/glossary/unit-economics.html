Одинична економіка (Unit Economics)

Продуктова стратегія та зростання

Одинична економіка (Unit Economics) — це аналіз доходів і витрат, пов'язаних з однією визначеною одиницею бізнесу, такою як клієнт, замовлення чи транзакція. Вона допомагає командам оцінити, чи генерує ця одиниця достатньо цінності для покриття пов'язаних із нею витрат.

What counts as a unit?

Choose the unit before choosing a formula. A paying account, an individual user, and a completed order answer different questions.

Business model Useful unit What to examine
Subscription software One paying customer account Acquisition cost, revenue, delivery costs, and retention over time.
Marketplace One completed transaction The platform's recognized revenue and the costs of facilitating the transaction.
AI workflow One completed customer task Allocated revenue and workflow costs, including failed and abandoned attempts.

For a marketplace, the total value customers transact, often called gross merchandise value (GMV), is not automatically the platform's revenue. Use the revenue the business actually recognizes under its model.

How do you calculate unit economics?

There is no single formula for every business. Two useful starting points are:

  • Contribution per order = net revenue per order − variable costs per order. State whether acquisition costs are included or reported separately.
  • Customer economics: compare customer acquisition cost with customer lifetime value, then check CAC payback.

Gross profit subtracts cost of goods sold or cost of revenue. Contribution margin subtracts the variable costs included in the analysis. These measures can differ because some delivery costs are fixed and some variable costs sit outside cost of revenue.

Document the cost categories and avoid counting an expense twice. Positive contribution still has to cover fixed expenses; positive lifetime customer economics does not establish company-wide profitability.

A SaaS unit economics example

Consider an illustrative subscription product. One paying account is one customer. Assume stable monthly revenue, gross margin, and customer churn, with no expansion revenue.

Input or result Amount
Acquisition spending attributable to 100 new accounts $100,000
CAC: $100,000 ÷ 100 $1,000
Monthly revenue per account $100
Monthly cost of revenue per account $20
Monthly gross profit per account $80
Gross margin: $80 ÷ $100 80%
Monthly customer churn 2%
Estimated gross-profit LTV: $100 × 0.80 ÷ 0.02 $4,000
LTV:CAC: $4,000 ÷ $1,000 4:1
Simplified CAC payback: $1,000 ÷ $80 12.5 months

The payback shortcut assumes an account continues generating $80 each month. Actual cohort payback must account for customers who leave. The LTV estimate also depends on its assumptions; it is not $4,000 of guaranteed profit from every account.

How does this affect product design?

Use the model to choose a measurable product question. If customers struggle to complete setup, investigate whether clearer onboarding helps suitable accounts reach their first useful result and become paying customers.

If the workflow requires repeated support intervention, examine the errors and handoffs behind that work. Measure task completion and support effort together before estimating a financial effect.

Compare customers acquired in the same period, called a cohort, and separate segments with different service requirements. A blended average can conceal a costly customer group.

What changes with AI products?

AI API charges can depend on input tokens, output tokens, caching, and tool use. An interface that encourages repeated generation can change delivery costs.

For example, an itinerary assistant might need three attempts and human review to produce one usable itinerary. Include those attempts and review work when estimating cost per completed task. Also include spending on tasks that never succeed within the measurement window. Define “usable” through explicit acceptance criteria. Test whether better inputs and clearer revision controls reduce avoidable retries while preserving output quality.
