EN 301 549

Design Systems & Engineering • UX & Product Architecture

EN 301 549 is a European technical standard for accessibility in information and communication technology (ICT). It covers web content, non-web documents, software, hardware, and services. Its web requirements draw on WCAG, and its other clauses address accessibility needs specific to different products and services.

How does EN 301 549 relate to WCAG?

WCAG provides testable requirements for accessible web content. EN 301 549 has separate clauses for web content, non-web documents, and software, as well as requirements for other areas such as documentation and support. If a service includes a downloadable PDF, for example, a team should assess the document as well as the page containing its download link.

Which version of EN 301 549 applies?

Status checked in November 2026.

Reference What to check
EN 301 549 v3.2.1 (2021) The harmonized reference cited for the EU Web Accessibility Directive. Its web criteria align with WCAG 2.1 Level AA. Use the applicable requirements identified for websites or mobile apps in Annex A.
EN 301 549 v4.1.1 (2026) ETSI published this edition in September 2026. Its web requirements draw on WCAG 2.2 Level AA. Publication of the standard and citation in the EU Official Journal are separate steps. Check the Commission’s list for the directive you are applying.
Contract or procurement Use the version and specific clauses named in the contract or tender. Confirm whether reports, documents, software, or support are in scope.

The Web Accessibility Directive (Directive (EU) 2016/2102) covers websites and mobile apps of EU public sector bodies. It also requires an accessibility statement and a feedback mechanism. The European Accessibility Act has its own scope and process for recognizing harmonized references.

Example. For a municipal booking portal, document the exact version, include the complete booking journey, and check whether its downloadable confirmation is in scope. Record the applicable clause alongside each finding, even when the same issue also maps to a WCAG criterion.
