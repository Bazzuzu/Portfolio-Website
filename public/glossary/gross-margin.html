Gross Margin

Product Strategy & Growth

Gross margin is the percentage of revenue remaining after subtracting cost of goods sold or cost of revenue. Gross profit is the corresponding dollar amount. Both measures describe delivery economics before other expenses are deducted.

How do you calculate gross margin?

Gross profit = revenue − cost of revenue.

Gross margin = gross profit ÷ revenue × 100.

Use revenue and costs from the same reporting period. Some reports use “gross margin” for a dollar subtotal; check whether a figure is an amount or a percentage. This article uses the percentage meaning.

If revenue is zero, the percentage is undefined. A negative gross profit with positive revenue produces a negative gross margin.

A SaaS gross margin example

An illustrative subscription account generates $100 in monthly revenue. Its assigned monthly cost of revenue is $20.

Calculation Result
Revenue $100
Cost of revenue $20
Gross profit: $100 − $20 $80
Gross margin: $80 ÷ $100 × 100 80%

For 100 identical accounts, revenue is $10,000, cost of revenue is $2,000, and gross profit is $8,000. The gross margin remains 80%.

The business still needs to cover other expenses, such as sales, marketing, general administration, and product development classified outside cost of revenue. An 80% gross margin is not an 80% net profit margin.

What belongs in SaaS cost of revenue?

Potential categories include production hosting, third-party services used to deliver the product, and customer support classified as delivery work. AI inference can be a delivery cost when the product relies on paid model calls.

The exact boundary depends on the company's accounting policies and operations. Ask finance which labor, infrastructure, payment, and software costs are included. Do not assume every engineering expense belongs in cost of revenue.

For an internal account-level analysis, also document how shared costs were assigned. A company-wide reported margin and a modeled margin for one customer segment may use different levels of allocation.

Gross margin vs. contribution margin

Gross margin follows the cost-of-revenue classification. Contribution margin subtracts variable costs.

A delivery cost can be fixed within a capacity range, while a sales commission can vary with sales outside cost of revenue. The two calculations can therefore differ even when they use the same revenue.

Keep the cost lists visible. Changing a classification or allocation can change a reported margin without changing the underlying customer experience or spending.

How can product designers use gross margin?

Consider a proposed AI feature for the $100 subscription. If it adds $15 in monthly delivery costs without additional revenue, gross profit falls from $80 to $65 and margin falls from 80% to 65%.

That is an illustrative scenario, not a reason to reject the feature automatically. Investigate the value it creates, whether customers will pay for it, and how it affects retention and service effort.

Test input quality, revision controls, and successful task completion alongside cost. Compare cost to serve across customer segments before attributing a company-wide margin change to one interface decision.
