OOUX Prototyping and Testing

UX & Product Architecture

OOUX prototyping and testing evaluates whether people can recognize a product's objects, understand their connections, and find the actions needed for a task. It uses a representation of the proposed product to investigate the assumptions in its object model and interface.

A prototype can start with simple linked records or low-fidelity screens. Add interaction detail when the research question requires it.

Choose the right level of detail

To examine structure, connect representative object views and include plausible content. To examine a consequential action, also represent its conditions, review, feedback, and relevant failure states.

A content prototype can test whether records fit the model. A clickable interface can test whether people understand where to go and what to do. Be explicit about which behavior has been simulated.

Example tasks for a booking prototype

Assume an operations tool contains Trip T-104 and Booking B-52.

Research question Task prompt
Object recognition “This supplier message refers to B-52. Find the matching record and tell me what it represents.”
Relationship understanding “Find the trip associated with this booking and identify its travelers.”
Action discovery “The traveler no longer needs this service. Show what you would do next.”
Outcome understanding After a submitted request: “What has happened, and what still needs to happen?”

Keep task wording neutral. Do not name a button or route when discovering that route is part of the question.

Include a direct entry from search, an empty related list, and an object state that changes available actions. Test with people who perform the relevant work.

What evidence should you record?

Observe mistaken identities, wrong turns, missing information, action misinterpretations, and recovery attempts. Note the starting conditions, task outcome, and supporting observations.

A participant can successfully reach a page while misunderstanding the record. Follow-up questions help investigate that distinction without teaching the answer during the task.

Separate an issue in the model from an issue in labels, placement, or prototype limitations. The same wrong turn can have different causes.

How to use the results

Revise the relevant object definition, relationship, attribute, action requirement, or representation. Retest the affected task and any important dependencies.

An OOUX-focused study can inform broader usability work. It does not by itself establish production performance, accessibility conformance, or support for untested workflows.
