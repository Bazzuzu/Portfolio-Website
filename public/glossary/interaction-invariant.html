Interaction Invariant

UX & Product Architecture • Design Systems & Engineering Established

Definition

An Interaction Invariant is a foundational interaction rule, gesture, or behavioral principle that remains completely uniform across every system state, module, and platform within a software suite.

Purpose

Fosters strong muscle memory and deep psychological trust, allowing users to safely predict system responses without mental recalculation.

Key Concepts

Behavioral constancyoperational determinismcognitive consistencyplatform invariants

How It Works

Designers codify universal interaction rules—such as uniform escape actions, persistent filtering logic, or standard keyboard bindings—that components cannot override.
