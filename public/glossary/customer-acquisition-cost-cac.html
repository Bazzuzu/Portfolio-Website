Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

Product Strategy & Growth

Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is the average cost of acquiring a new customer. For the SaaS model used here, it equals acquisition-related sales and marketing costs divided by the number of new paying customer accounts acquired.

How do you calculate CAC?

CAC = acquisition-related sales and marketing costs ÷ new paying customers acquired.

Define what counts as a customer and align the costs with the acquisition process. In B2B SaaS, count a paying company account once; its invited users are not separate acquired customers.

A practical calculation needs a documented cost boundary:

Include in acquisition spending Handle separately
Advertising, acquisition content, and campaign agencies Product development and ongoing service delivery.
Acquisition-related sales and marketing salaries, benefits, and commissions Customer retention and account expansion work.
Acquisition tools and the acquisition share of shared expenses Free signups and leads, which are not paying customers in this calculation.

Allocate mixed activities consistently. For example, a team member who handles both new sales and renewals should not have their entire cost automatically assigned to new customer acquisition.

If no paying customers were acquired, the denominator is zero. Report that CAC is undefined for that calculation and show the spending and customer count separately.

A CAC calculation example

An illustrative SaaS business attributes these costs to acquiring 100 new paying accounts:

Cost Amount
Paid campaigns $40,000
Acquisition-related sales and marketing compensation $45,000
Acquisition tools, content, and agency work $15,000
Total $100,000

CAC = $100,000 ÷ 100 = $1,000 per new paying account.

Using only advertising spend would produce $400 per account. Label that narrower measure as ad spend per acquired account; it excludes $60,000 of acquisition costs.

If the same campaign also generates 1,000 free signups, dividing the full $100,000 by signups gives $100 per signup. It does not change the paying-customer CAC to $100.

CAC vs. CPA and blended vs. paid CAC

Cost per acquisition or action (CPA) depends on the conversion event being counted. A campaign's event might be a lead, signup, or purchase. CPA matches CAC only when the customer definition and cost scope match.

Blended CAC combines acquisition channels. A paid-channel CAC calculation focuses on customers attributed to paid channels and the costs assigned to them. State the attribution method, handling of shared costs, and treatment of customers who encounter several channels.

Organic acquisition can involve content, staff, and tools. A customer arriving without a paid ad does not establish that acquiring them cost nothing.

What makes a CAC estimate misleading?

Timing matters. Spending in January may acquire accounts in March. For longer sales cycles, use an appropriate lag or cohort model rather than assuming this month's wins came from this month's spending.

Also check whether the result mixes sales-assisted enterprise accounts with self-serve customers. Report comparable segments before interpreting a change.

There is no universal good dollar value for CAC. Read it alongside LTV:CAC, payback period, retention, and delivery costs.

How can product designers use CAC?

Investigate where suitable prospects fail to understand the offer, complete a trial task, or become paying accounts. Pricing clarity, accessible forms, and an effective first-use experience give the team specific hypotheses to test.

A higher trial-to-paid conversion rate could lower CAC if acquisition spending and customer quality stay comparable. Check retained paying accounts and refunds as well. Changing the channel mix or admitting poorly matched customers can make a conversion increase misleading.
