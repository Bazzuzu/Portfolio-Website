Core Model

UX & Product Architecture • Product Strategy & Growth

The Core Model is Are Halland’s method for planning a page or digital experience around the overlap between user tasks and business goals. It connects the content people need with the paths that bring them there and the useful next steps they can take.

What does a core model contain?

Choose a core page and record its user tasks, business goals, inward paths, core content, and forward paths. These fields help a team decide what the experience must support before working on its layout.

Example: A hotel room detail page

Core Model field Example
Core page Details for a family room.
User tasks Check capacity, bed arrangements, cancellation terms, and availability for the intended dates.
Business goals Help travelers choose a suitable room and complete a booking.
Inward paths A room search result, a saved link, or a search-engine result.
Core content Room capacity, bed types, photos, applicable terms, and availability and price for selected dates.
Forward paths Select dates, book the room, compare another room, or ask a question.

A traveler entering from a saved link needs enough context to identify the room and select dates. Someone arriving from dated search results expects the price and availability to match those dates. The model makes these entry conditions explicit.

How do you use the Core Model?

Develop the model with people responsible for user research, content, product, and business outcomes. Use research to establish user tasks, then sketch how the page can support them and the intended next steps.

Test the proposed experience through realistic entry paths. Can a traveler confirm the room fits their party and find the applicable cancellation terms? Can they proceed with their chosen dates? Revisit the model when the test reveals missing information or an onward path that interrupts the task.
