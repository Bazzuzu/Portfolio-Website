AI-assisted Design

AI & Agentic Systems • UX & Product Architecture

AI-assisted design is the use of AI tools to support parts of a design process, such as exploring alternatives, drafting content, or building prototypes. The designer remains responsible for deciding which problem to address and evaluating the result.

Designing with AI vs. designing an AI product

AI-assisted design describes how the work is done. AI-native design concerns products whose experience depends substantially on AI capabilities.

A designer can use AI to prototype a conventional settings page. A team can also design an AI assistant using sketches and ordinary prototyping tools. These are different dimensions of the work.

Where can AI help a designer?

Task Useful contribution What still needs judgment
Explore alternatives Draft different layouts or approaches Whether they address a real need
Write interface content Suggest labels and error messages Accuracy, tone, and the actual recovery path
Build a prototype Implement a specified interaction Whether the behavior represents the idea being tested
Review a design Identify possible omissions Whether each finding is supported and important

Treat generated alternatives as proposals. Repetition across several responses is not independent evidence.

Example: an operations dashboard

Suppose a designer has identified the core objects, statuses, and relationships in a booking workflow. They give an agent that model, representative content, and the shared component references.

The agent builds a prototype with sorting and filtering. The designer checks whether the filters refer to the right objects, whether important exceptions remain visible, and whether the UI fits the actual task.

Starting from a verified object model gives the prototype a clearer structure. It does not establish that users will understand or value the proposed dashboard.

A practical AI-assisted workflow

Define the question and constraints first. Supply the evidence, source material, component references, and examples needed for that question. Ask for a bounded deliverable that can be inspected.

Review the result against the brief. Check factual claims against sources, behavior against requirements, and implementation against the real component API. Revise or discard proposals that do not hold up.

Then test the design with appropriate users or other evidence. Record which conclusions come from research and which remain assumptions.

Include review, correction, and maintenance when assessing effort. Producing a screen quickly is one part of the work, not a complete measure of the design process.
