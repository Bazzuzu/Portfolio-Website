ADA Web Accessibility

Design Systems & Engineering • UX & Product Architecture

ADA web accessibility refers to providing people with disabilities access to online services under the Americans with Disabilities Act. Title II applies to U.S. state and local government entities. Title III applies to businesses and nonprofits that qualify as places of public accommodation.

How do Title II and Title III differ?

ADA title Web accessibility requirements
Title II The U.S. Department of Justice rule sets WCAG 2.1 Level AA as the technical standard for covered web content and mobile apps that state and local governments provide or make available, including through relevant vendor arrangements. The rule specifies exceptions and other conditions.
Title III The Department of Justice’s web guidance explains that covered public accommodations must make the goods and services they offer online accessible. Use WCAG as a technical benchmark and evaluate the obligations that apply to the business.

What are the ADA Title II web rule deadlines?

Under the April 2026 interim final rule, state and local government entities with a population of 50,000 or more, excluding special districts, have until April 26, 2027. Entities below 50,000 and special district governments have until April 26, 2028. Existing Title II obligations continue to apply before these dates.

What should a product team do?

For a city’s online permit application, include the portal, its forms, and the submission and payment steps in the accessibility scope. Identify any applicable exceptions, then evaluate the covered process against WCAG 2.1 AA. For a private retailer, start with the scope of Title III and the applicable legal context. Use WCAG to turn barriers in browsing and checkout into testable findings. Section 508 is a separate federal ICT framework relevant to U.S. federal agencies and the products they procure.
