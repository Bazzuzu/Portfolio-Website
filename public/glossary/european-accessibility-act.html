European Accessibility Act (EAA)

Design Systems & Engineering • UX & Product Architecture

The European Accessibility Act (EAA) is an EU directive that sets accessibility requirements for certain products and services sold or provided in the EU. It covers consumer e-commerce, consumer banking, e-books, and specified digital aspects of passenger transport, among other services. The directive sets June 28, 2025, as the start date for applying national measures, with transition provisions for some cases.

Which websites and apps does the EAA cover?

Scope depends on the service being offered. For e-commerce, the directive covers online services offered to consumers with a view to concluding a consumer contract. Websites and apps are channels through which those services can be offered. For websites and mobile apps of EU public sector bodies, see the Web Accessibility Directive and its relationship to EN 301 549.

Article 4(5) exempts microenterprises providing services. The definition in Article 3(23) uses fewer than 10 people and either annual turnover or an annual balance sheet total of no more than €2 million. The directive treats microenterprises dealing with products differently. Check the national implementing law and any relevant transition provisions for the service and country.

What should a product team check in checkout?

For a travel site selling bookings to consumers, start with the real journey from fare selection to purchase confirmation. Test choosing a fare without a mouse, identifying errors in the traveler form, and completing payment authentication with assistive technology. Include payment or identity steps supplied by third parties in the evaluation. Annex I specifically addresses accessibility of identification, security, and payment functions provided as part of an e-commerce service.

How does the EAA relate to WCAG and EN 301 549?

The EAA sets the legal requirements. WCAG gives teams testable web criteria, and EN 301 549 covers a broader range of information and communication technology. Article 15 ties a presumption of conformity to requirements covered by standards or parts of standards referenced for the directive in the EU Official Journal. For an assessment, identify the applicable national law, Annex I requirements, and any references published for the EAA.
